R-EHD-01: The maker is ready to be an EHDS data holder

SHOULD · Level 2 (Forward-compliant) · Users: all · Detection: manual · Effort: medium

Requirement. The maker knows whether it will be a health data holder under the EHDS, can describe its datasets and has a plan for answering health data access bodies, including for wellness data.

Why. EHDS secondary use rules (from 2029 and 2031) cover data from wellness applications too; the EDPB and EDPS asked for limits on that.

How to improve. Write a short dataset description and a contact process for data access bodies.

Sources. EHDS, EDPB-EDPS-EHDS

W-DAT-01: Data from wearables and sensors is accessible to the user

MUST · Level 1 (Legal baseline) · Users: all · Features: wearables, sensors · Detection: manual · Effort: medium

Requirement. Users can get the data their connected device or related service generates, easily, securely and free. They can have it shared with a third party they choose. Products placed on the market from 12 Sep 2026 make this possible by design.

Why. Data Act Art. 3 to 5, applicable from 12 Sep 2025 (design duty from 12 Sep 2026).

How to improve. Offer a data download and a documented sharing route; tell users before purchase what data the product generates.

Sources. DATA-ACT

W-DAT-02: Reproductive, sexual and mental health data stays on the device by default

MUST · Level 2 (Forward-compliant) · Users: all · Features: reproductive_health, mental_health, genetic · Detection: manual · Effort: high

Requirement. Cycle, fertility, pregnancy, sexual health, mental health and genetic data is stored on the device by default. Cloud sync and backup are opt-in, end-to-end encrypted where possible and explained.

Why. These are the most sensitive health data and the most often misused (GDPR Art. 9). Apps for depression were found sending data to large platforms without saying so (Huckvale 2019).

How to improve. Make local storage the default; add an explained opt-in for sync with end-to-end encryption.

Sources. GDPR, R-HUCKVALE2019

W-EHD-01: Interoperability with health records is claimed only with the EHDS label

MUST · Level 2 (Forward-compliant) · Users: all · Detection: manifest · Effort: high

Requirement. If the product claims it works with electronic health record systems, it meets the EHDS essential requirements and common specifications, carries the EHDS label, informs users of what the interoperability means, and is registered in the EU database.

Why. EHDS Art. 47 to 49 (labelling of wellness applications); applies in stages from 26 Mar 2027.

How to improve. Either drop the claim or plan the EHDS conformity work and label; record it in hdsf.yaml (ehds).

Sources. EHDS

W-EHD-02: Data goes to a health record only when the person chooses

MUST · Level 2 (Forward-compliant) · Users: all · Detection: manual · Effort: medium

Requirement. Being interoperable never means automatic sharing. Each transfer of wellness data to an EHR system, or to a professional, happens only after the person chooses it, and they can choose which data.

Why. EHDS Art. 48(2). The EDPB and EDPS asked for strong limits on the use of wellness data (Joint Opinion 03/2022).

How to improve. Add a share screen with data choices; log each share; allow stopping future shares.

Sources. EHDS, EDPB-EDPS-EHDS

W-QUA-01: A wellness product stays wellness

MUST · Level 1 (Legal baseline) · Users: all · Detection: manual · Effort: low

Requirement. The product does not add features that serve a medical purpose for an individual (diagnosis, disease monitoring, dose calculation, treatment decisions) without a new qualification. General fitness, sleep, diet and wellbeing features are fine.

Why. MDCG 2019-11 rev. 1 explains when software for lifestyle or wellbeing remains outside the MDR, and when disease prevention brings it under Rule 11.

How to improve. Keep a list of feature ideas that would need re-qualification; route them through CORE-QUA-04.

Sources. MDR, MDCG-2019-11

W-QUA-02: Wellness texts make no medical claims

MUST · Level 1 (Legal baseline) · Users: all · Detection: static · Effort: low

Requirement. Store texts, screens and notifications of a wellness product do not say it diagnoses, detects, treats or cures a disease, or that it is "medical grade" or "clinically proven", unless that is true and qualified.

Why. Claims make the intended purpose (MDR; MDCG 2019-11). MDCG 2025-4 asks platforms to list wellness apps apart from medical device apps.

How to improve. Search the texts for medical claims before each release and rewrite them.

Sources. MDR, MDCG-2019-11, MDCG-2025-4

W-SEC-01: Connected wellness products meet the Cyber Resilience Act

MUST · Level 2 (Forward-compliant) · Users: all · Features: wearables, sensors · Detection: manual · Effort: high

Requirement. Connected wellness devices and their apps meet the CRA essential requirements by 11 Dec 2027: secure by default, security updates, vulnerability handling and reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities from 11 Sep 2026.

Why. CRA; medical devices are outside it, wellness products with digital elements generally are not.

How to improve. Run a CRA gap analysis; decide the conformity route and support period.

Sources. CRA
