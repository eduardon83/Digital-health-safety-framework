R-CLN-01: Clinical investigations of devices follow the MDR

MUST · Level 1 (Legal baseline) · Users: patients (people under care or treatment), public (people managing their own health or wellbeing) · Detection: manual · Effort: high

Requirement. A study to gather clinical evidence for medical device software follows MDR Chapter VI: protocol, ethics committee opinion, competent authority notification or authorisation, informed consent and safety reporting.

Why. MDR Art. 62 to 82.

How to improve. Plan the investigation with the competent authority's procedure (INFARMED in Portugal).

Sources. MDR

R-DAT-01: Research uses have a legal basis and approval

MUST · Level 1 (Legal baseline) · Users: all · Features: research_use · Detection: manifest · Effort: medium

Requirement. Each research use of health data has an Art. 6 basis, an Art. 9(2) condition (often (j) with national safeguards, or explicit consent), ethics approval where required, and a protocol. Research use is not hidden inside the terms of service.

Why. GDPR Art. 9(2)(j) and 89. The EDPB's draft research guidelines (1/2026) are under consultation.

How to improve. Record each study in hdsf.yaml (research) with basis, approval and protocol.

Sources. GDPR, EDPB-RESEARCH

R-DAT-02: Care data and research data are kept apart

MUST · Level 1 (Legal baseline) · Users: all · Detection: manual · Effort: medium

Requirement. Researchers receive pseudonymised extracts with only the fields the protocol needs, in a separate environment. The re-identification key stays with the care system.

Why. GDPR Art. 25 and 89(1); EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on pseudonymisation; CJEU C-413/23 P.

How to improve. Build an extract pipeline with field selection and codes; restrict research environments.

Sources. GDPR, EDPB-PSEUDO, CJEU-SRB

R-DAT-03: Only group results are published

MUST · Level 2 (Forward-compliant) · Users: all · Detection: manual · Effort: medium

Requirement. Published results and open datasets hold group figures only, with small groups suppressed or merged, and are checked for re-identification before release.

Why. GDPR Art. 89; EHDS secondary use allows only anonymised or pseudonymised data in secure processing environments.

How to improve. Set minimum group sizes; add a disclosure check to the publication step.

Sources. GDPR, EHDS, EDPB-ANON

R-EHD-01: The maker is ready to be an EHDS data holder

SHOULD · Level 2 (Forward-compliant) · Users: all · Detection: manual · Effort: medium

Requirement. The maker knows whether it will be a health data holder under the EHDS, can describe its datasets and has a plan for answering health data access bodies, including for wellness data.

Why. EHDS secondary use rules (from 2029 and 2031) cover data from wellness applications too; the EDPB and EDPS asked for limits on that.

How to improve. Write a short dataset description and a contact process for data access bodies.

Sources. EHDS, EDPB-EDPS-EHDS
