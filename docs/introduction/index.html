HDSF v0.2.0 guide

Introduction

The Health Data Safety Framework (HDSF) is an open set of checkable rules for digital health products used in the European Union. It covers wellness and lifestyle apps, medical device software, electronic health record systems, research platforms and telehealth.

HDSF is a draft for public comment. It is not legal or regulatory advice.

Why HDSF

Digital health sits where several EU laws meet. The GDPR (RGPD in Portuguese) treats health data as a special category. The MDR and IVDR decide whether software is a medical device. The European Health Data Space (EHDS) adds rules for health records and for wellness apps that connect to them, applying in stages from 2027. The AI Act, the Cyber Resilience Act, NIS2, the Data Act and the new Product Liability Directive add more. Each law is clear on its own; together they are hard to follow, above all for small teams.

The evidence shows why it matters. A study of 15,838 free health apps found that 28% had no privacy policy, and only 47% of the data they sent matched the policy they had (Tangari 2021). Of 36 top apps for depression and smoking cessation, 29 sent data to Facebook or Google services, and only 12 said so (Huckvale 2019).

What HDSF gives you

  • Rules (catalogue). Each rule says what to do, why (with its legal or other source), how

to improve, how much effort it takes, and how it is checked.

  • A first question: is your software a medical device? (guide). Every other answer depends on it.
  • A regulatory map (guide): which law applies to which kind of product, and from when.
  • A GDPR (RGPD) map (page): each GDPR article that matters for health products, and the rules that apply it.
  • A Workbench for designers, product owners, decision makers and researchers: describe the product, add the documents, and get

checks, a document review, design recommendations and reports in the browser. No code, nothing uploaded.

  • A manifest, hdsf.yaml (template), that describes the product in one page.
  • A scanner that reads the manifest and the code and writes reports: an interactive HTML dashboard, PDF, Word, Markdown, JSON

and SARIF (how to use it).

  • Checklists for a self-assessment by hand (self-assessment).
  • Sources with dates and their verification status (legislation and evidence).

Profiles

Every product meets the core rules. Then it adds the profiles that fit; a product can have several.

ProfileForExamples
coreEvery digital health productAnything that processes health data
WWellness and lifestyle, no medical purposeFitness, sleep, diet, mood, cycle tracking
MMedical device software (MDR or IVDR)Symptom checkers that suggest a diagnosis, insulin dose calculators, AI that reads scans
EEHR systems and the EHDSClinic and hospital record systems, patient portals
RResearch and secondary useStudy platforms, registries, data sharing for research
TTelehealth and care deliveryVideo consultations, remote monitoring services, care platforms

Rules also depend on who uses the product (the public, patients, professionals, children) and on its features (wearables, AI, chat, research use and others).

Three layers

HDSF works in three layers, as CSEF does. Each rule has a level.

LevelLayerWhat it rests on
1Legal baselineLaw in force or adopted: GDPR (RGPD), MDR and IVDR, AI Act, DSA, ePrivacy, NIS2, Data Act, national law. Meet it now.
2EU recommendations and forward complianceLaw that applies later (EHDS, CRA, AI Act high-risk dates) and what EU bodies recommend: EDPB guidelines and opinions on AI models, web scraping, consent and deceptive design; Commission guidance and recommendations (EHR exchange format, clear writing); WHO guidance on AI for health; harmonised standards. Plan for it.
3Best practice from researchPeer-reviewed research and quality frameworks on design, UX, aesthetics, risk communication, health literacy and evidence (for example MARS, icon arrays, consent design studies, HLS-EU, NICE). What good products do beyond the law.

MUST rules are required at their level. SHOULD rules are strongly recommended; skip one only with a recorded reason.

Children

Health products used by children also apply the Child-Safe EdTech Framework (CSEF). HDSF and CSEF share the same engine, so their reports look alike and can be read together.

How HDSF is made

HDSF is maintained by Kendir Studios with contributors, under CC BY 4.0 (content) and Apache-2.0 (code). Every source has a "verified" date or is marked "pending"; pending entries are listed first in the legislation page and need a check. Contributions are welcome, above all national notes, checked sources and real product cases.
